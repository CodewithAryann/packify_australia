That steam condenses on the lid, drips back onto the glaze, and turns a crisp shell into a sticky film within about twelve minutes of closing. Donut box vent holes solve this, and their placement follows a consistent pattern across well-designed bakery boxes.

Standard specification: 0.25-inch diameter circular perforations, spaced 2 inches on center, positioned in the lid panel and along the upper third of the two long side walls. A dozen box carries eight to ten vents: six across the lid, two per long wall. A 24-count carries fourteen to sixteen. The 6-count needs four to six, since its sealed air volume keeps condensation below saturation.

Vents belong in the upper third of side walls, never the lower third. Low vents sit level with the glaze pool on the bottom board and leak sugar syrup onto a customer’s car seat. Keep vents at least 0.75 inches from any score line, since perforating a fold weakens it and the panel tears when a driver grabs the box by one edge. Never vent the bottom panel: grease migration through a bottom perforation is the most common cause of blowout on a stacked delivery order.5:T415,Board selection determines whether a box looks clean at the twenty-minute mark or shows translucent oil spots. 18pt SBS with a clay coat on the print side handles 6-count and 12-count boxes. It holds a crisp fold, prints cleanly at 300 lpi, and resists grease strike-through for the two to four hour window that covers retail and most delivery routes. 24pt SBS or E-flute corrugated cardboard is the correct call for 24-count boxes, where the 18-inch lid span needs the additional caliper or flute structure, otherwise the lid sags into the top row of donuts.

Grease-resistant treatment should be a PFAS-free barrier coating. California, New York, Washington, Minnesota, and Colorado have all restricted intentionally added PFAS in food packaging, and those rules cover donut boxes exactly as they cover fry cartons. Ask for documentation before the first order, not after the first inspection. Standard folding cartons in an auto-lock bottom style assemble in under three seconds each, which matters when open requires forty boxes built ahead.6:T584,Delivery changes the requirements in three specific ways.

Stacking strength becomes the binding constraint. An 18pt SBS dozen box supports approximately 8 to 10 pounds of top load before the lid deflects into the product. Four stacked full boxes weigh about 7 pounds, leaving almost no margin. Ghost kitchens running high delivery volume should specify 20pt board or an E-flute variant.

Tamper evidence is now a platform requirement, not an option. A perforated tuck flap or a printed seal label satisfies DoorDash and Uber Eats tamper policies without adding a separate sealing step to the pack line.

Interior legibility replaces the counter conversation. In delivery, the box is the only communication. A printed interior grid with position numbers, or a checkbox list inside the lid flap, resolves the most common delivery complaint: a customer believing an item was missing when it was simply unrecognizable.

A printed lid travels to an office break room and sits in front of thirty people for four hours, which is why print pays back faster on donut boxes than on almost any other bakery box. Eco-friendly packaging in uncoated kraft with a single-color flexo print captures most of that return at the lowest per-unit cost.
